Whenua ideas

Got an idea for our whenua? Think it through first

Every good idea is welcome. These questions help you work out what needs thinking about before you bring it to whānau. You do not need all the answers. "I don't know yet" is a useful answer, because it shows what to find out next.

Nothing you type here is saved to the site. Copy it into a new Forum topic, an email or a document when you are ready to share it, or print it to take to a hui.

1

The idea

Give it a short name. For example: "Whānau campsite near the river" or "Plant a rongoā garden". Keep it simple.
2

Who it is for

Think about income, housing, kai, jobs, learning, getting home to the whenua, or looking after the land. Which branches or generations would feel it most?
3

Where on the whenua

Roughly where, and how big. Is it one block or the whole whenua? Is the land flat, wet, steep, covered in bush or already used for something?
4

Land status and rules

Who owns it and in what shares? Are there leases, covenants or rights of way already? Would the council need to approve it (resource consent, building consent)? If you do not know, say so. That is a fine answer and a good first thing to find out.
5

Access and services

Road and parking, power, water, toilets or wastewater, phone or internet. Would anything need to be built or upgraded?
6

Rough costs and money

A ballpark is enough. What would you need to buy or build? Who might pay (whānau contributions, grants, a loan)? Could it earn money, and who would that money go to?
7

People and time

Who would actually do the work week to week? What skills do you have, and which do you need? Be honest about how much time people can give.
8

Risks and impact

Think about neighbours, awa and waterways, native bush, wāhi tapu, and the effect on other owners. What would you do to reduce each risk?
9

Tikanga and whānau

Which kaumātua or branch representatives should hear it before the wider whānau? How does it show manaakitanga and kaitiakitanga, and leave the whenua in good shape for the next generations?
10

First steps

A trial, a working bee, a quote, a conversation with the council. What could happen in the next three months, and what would tell us it is worth carrying on?
11

What you need from whānau

Feedback, a decision in principle, volunteers, money, or just permission to look into it further?

This is a thinking tool, not legal or financial advice. Rules about Māori land, council consents and money can be tricky. Before spending any money or making promises, check the details with the right people, such as a lawyer, the Māori Land Court, the council, or an accountant.
